### Biot-Savart's Law
#magnetostatics/biot-savart #magnetic-field

> **Biot-Savart's Law** is the fundamental law of magnetostatics that describes the magnetic field generated by a steady electric current. It is analogous to Coulomb's Law in electrostatics, as it provides a method to calculate the magnetic field from its source (current) for any arbitrary conductor shape.

The law states that the differential magnetic flux density ($d\vec{B}$) at a point P due to a differential current element ($I d\vec{l}$) is:
$$\boxed{\quad d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu I}{4\pi} \frac{d\vec{l} \times \hat{a}_R}{R^2} \quad}$$
where:
-   $d\vec{B}$ is the differential magnetic flux density vector (in Tesla, T).
-   $\mu$ is the permeability of the medium ($\mu = \mu_0 \mu_r$).
-   $I$ is the steady current in the conductor (in Amperes, A).
-   $d\vec{l}$ is the differential length vector of the conductor, pointing in the direction of the current.
-   $\hat{a}_R$ is the unit vector pointing from the current element to the point P.
-   $R$ is the distance from the current element to the point P.

The direction of the magnetic field is perpendicular to the plane containing both $d\vec{l}$ and $\hat{a}_R$, as determined by the right-hand rule for the cross product.

To find the total magnetic field $\vec{B}$ at point P, we must integrate $d\vec{B}$ over the entire length of the current-carrying conductor:
$$\vec{B} = \int_L \frac{\mu I}{4\pi} \frac{d\vec{l} \times \hat{a}_R}{R^2}$$

### Applications of Biot-Savart's Law
#biot-savart/applications

Biot-Savart's law can be used to derive the magnetic field for several standard configurations.

##### 1. Magnetic Field of a Finite Straight Conductor
For a straight wire of finite length carrying current $I$, the magnetic field at a perpendicular distance $\rho$ from the wire is:
$$\boxed{\quad B = \frac{\mu I}{4\pi \rho} (\sin\alpha_2 + \sin\alpha_1) \quad}$$
where $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ are the angles subtended at the point P by the ends of the wire with respect to the perpendicular from P.

##### 2. Magnetic Field of an Infinite Straight Conductor
This is a special case of the finite conductor where the ends extend to infinity, so $\alpha_1 \to 90^\circ$ and $\alpha_2 \to 90^\circ$.
$$\begin{align}
B &= \frac{\mu I}{4\pi \rho} (\sin 90^\circ + \sin 90^\circ) \\
 &= \frac{\mu I}{4\pi \rho} (1 + 1)
\end{align}$$
$$\boxed{\quad B = \frac{\mu I}{2\pi \rho} \quad}$$
This result is more easily obtained using [[Ampere's Circuital Law|Ampere's Law]] due to the high symmetry.

##### 3. Magnetic Field on the Axis of a Circular Loop
For a circular current loop of radius $a$ lying in the xy-plane, the magnetic field at a point P on the z-axis (at a distance $z$ from the center) is directed along the z-axis and has a magnitude of:
$$\boxed{\quad B_z = \frac{\mu I a^2}{2(a^2 + z^2)^{3/2}} \quad}$$

##### 4. Magnetic Field at the Center of a Circular Loop
This is a special case of the above formula where the point P is at the center of the loop ($z=0$).
$$\boxed{\quad B_z = \frac{\mu I}{2a} \quad}$$
