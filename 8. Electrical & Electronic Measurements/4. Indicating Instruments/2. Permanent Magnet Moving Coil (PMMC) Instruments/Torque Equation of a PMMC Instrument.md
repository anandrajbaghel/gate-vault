### Torque Equation of a PMMC Instrument
#pmmc #torque-equation #indicating-instruments

> The torque equation of a Permanent Magnet Moving Coil (PMMC) instrument describes the relationship between the electrical current flowing through the coil and the mechanical deflection of its pointer. The final, steady-state position of the pointer is reached when the deflecting torque, caused by the current, is exactly balanced by the controlling torque, provided by the springs.

#### Deflecting Torque ($T_d$)
#deflecting-torque

The deflecting torque is generated by the Lorentz force acting on the sides of the current-carrying coil placed in the magnetic field of the permanent magnet.
The force on one side of the coil is given by:
$$ F = N B I l $$
where $N$ is the number of turns, $B$ is the magnetic flux density, $I$ is the current, and $l$ is the length of the coil side.
Since there are two sides of the coil experiencing this force in opposite directions, they form a couple. The torque produced by this couple is the force multiplied by the perpendicular distance between the forces, which is the width of the coil ($w$).
$$\begin{align}
T_d &= F \times w \\
&= (N B I l) \times w \\
&= N B (l \times w) I
\end{align}$$
Since the area of the coil is $A = l \times w$, the deflecting torque is:
$$\boxed{\quad T_d = N B A I \quad}$$
The term $G = NBA$ is called the **galvanometer constant**. So, $T_d = G I$. This shows that the deflecting torque in a PMMC instrument is directly proportional to the current flowing through its coil.
$$ T_d \propto I $$

#### Controlling Torque ($T_c$)
#controlling-torque

PMMC instruments use [[Types of Controlling Torques|spring control]]. The controlling torque provided by the two hairsprings is directly proportional to the angle of deflection, $\theta$.
$$\boxed{\quad T_c = K_s \theta \quad}$$
where $K_s$ is the combined stiffness or spring constant of the springs. The controlling torque opposes the deflecting torque.
$$ T_c \propto \theta $$

#### Final Deflection and Scale
#pmmc/scale

The pointer of the instrument comes to a steady position when the deflecting torque and controlling torque are equal.
$$ T_d = T_c $$
Substituting the expressions for the torques:
$$ N B A I = K_s \theta $$
Solving for the deflection angle $\theta$:
$$\boxed{\quad \theta = \left(\frac{N B A}{K_s}\right) I \quad}$$
All the terms in the bracket are constants for a given instrument. Therefore, the deflection angle is directly proportional to the current.
$$\boxed{\quad \theta \propto I \quad}$$
This direct proportionality between the deflection and the current is the reason why **PMMC instruments have a uniform (linear) scale**.

#### Dynamic Equation of Motion
#instrument-dynamics

The steady-state equation describes the final position of the pointer. The transient behavior, as the pointer moves from zero to its final position, is described by a second-order differential equation that includes the effects of inertia and damping.
$$ J\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} + D\frac{d\theta}{dt} + K_s\theta = T_d $$
where,
-   $J$ is the moment of inertia of the entire moving system.
-   $D$ is the damping coefficient from the [[Types of Damping Torques|eddy current damping]].
-   $K_s\theta$ is the controlling torque ($T_c$).
-   $T_d$ is the deflecting torque.
