**Q.** What does Thevenin's theorem state?
?
Thevenin's theorem states that any linear electrical network with voltage and current sources can be replaced by an equivalent circuit consisting of a single voltage source ($V_{th}$) in series with a single resistor ($R_{th}$).

#electric-circuits / [[Network Theorems]]

**Q.** What is the significance of the per-unit system in power system analysis?
?
The per-unit system simplifies power system analysis by normalizing all electrical quantities to a common base value. This makes calculations easier, especially in systems with multiple voltage levels and transformers.

#power-system / [[Per-Unit System]]

**Q.** What are the different types of buses in a power system load flow study?
?
In a load flow study, buses are typically categorized into three types: the Slack Bus (or Swing Bus), Generator Buses (or PV buses), and Load Buses (or PQ buses).

#power-system / [[Bus Classification (Slack, PQ, PV)]]

**Q.** What is the function of a transformer in an electrical power system?
?
A transformer is a static electrical device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another through electromagnetic induction. It is commonly used to step up or step down voltage levels.

#electrical-machines / [[Transformers]]

---
**Q.** What is the slip of an induction motor?
?
The slip of an induction motor is the difference between the synchronous speed of the magnetic field and the actual rotor speed, expressed as a percentage of the synchronous speed.

#electrical-machines / [[Induction Motors]]

---
**Q.** What is Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL)?
?
Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) states that the algebraic sum of currents entering a node (or a closed boundary) is zero. This law is based on the conservation of charge.

#electric-circuits / [[Kirchhoff's Laws]]

---
**Q.** What is Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL)?
?
Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) states that the algebraic sum of all voltages around any closed loop in a circuit is equal to zero. This law is based on the conservation of energy.

#electric-circuits / [[Kirchhoff's Laws]]

---
**Q.** What is the purpose of a synchronous condenser?
?
A synchronous condenser is an over-excited synchronous motor running at no load. Its primary function is to absorb or supply reactive power to a power system, thereby regulating the voltage.

#electrical-machines / [[Synchronous Machines]]

---
**Q.** What is the stability of a power system?
?
Power system stability refers to the ability of the system to return to a state of equilibrium after being subjected to a disturbance, such as a fault or a sudden change in load.

#power-system / [[Classification of Power System Stability|Power System Stability]]

**Q.** What is the difference between a DC generator and a DC motor?
?
A DC generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy, while a DC motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. However, their basic construction is the same.

#electrical-machines / [[DC Machines]]

**Q.** What is the Z-transform?
?
The Z-transform is a mathematical tool used for the analysis of discrete-time signals and systems. It converts a discrete-time signal, which is a sequence of real or complex numbers, into a complex frequency-domain representation.

#signals-and-systems / [[The Z-Transform]]

---
**Q.** What is the Laplace transform?
?
The Laplace transform is a mathematical tool used to convert a function of a real variable (often time) to a function of a complex variable (complex frequency). It is widely used in the analysis of linear time-invariant systems.

#signals-and-systems / [[The Laplace Transform]]

---
**Q.** What is a Fourier series?
?
A Fourier series is a way of representing a periodic function as a sum of sine and cosine functions. It is a powerful tool for analyzing periodic signals.

#mathematics / [[Fourier Series]]

---
**Q.** What is the Nyquist sampling theorem?
?
The Nyquist sampling theorem states that for a band-limited signal to be perfectly reconstructed from its samples, the sampling frequency must be at least twice the highest frequency component of the signal.

#signals-and-systems / [[Sampling]]

---
**Q.** What is a diode?
?
A diode is a two-terminal electronic component that conducts current primarily in one direction. It has low resistance to current flow in one direction and high resistance in the other.

#electric-circuits / [[Diodes]]

---
**Q.** What is a transistor?
?
A transistor is a semiconductor device used to amplify or switch electronic signals and electrical power. It is a fundamental building block of modern electronic devices.

#electric-circuits / [[Transistors]]

---
**Q.** What is an operational amplifier (op-amp)?
?
An operational amplifier (op-amp) is a high-gain electronic voltage amplifier with a differential input and, usually, a single-ended output. It is a versatile building block for analog circuits.

#electric-circuits / [[Operational Amplifiers]]

---
**Q.** What is Norton's theorem?
?
Norton's theorem states that any linear electrical network can be replaced by an equivalent circuit consisting of a single current source (IN) in parallel with a single resistor (RN).

#electric-circuits / [[Network Theorems]]

---
**Q.** What is the maximum power transfer theorem?
?
The maximum power transfer theorem states that to obtain maximum external power from a source with a finite internal resistance, the resistance of the load must equal the resistance of the source as viewed from its output terminals.

#electric-circuits / [[Network Theorems]]

---
**Q.** What is resonance in an RLC circuit?
?
Resonance in an RLC circuit is a condition where the inductive reactance and capacitive reactance are equal, resulting in a purely resistive impedance. This leads to maximum current for a series RLC circuit and minimum current for a parallel RLC circuit.

#electric-circuits / [[Resonance]]

---
**Q.** What is a transfer function?
?
A transfer function is a mathematical representation of the relationship between the input and output of a linear time-invariant system in the frequency domain.

#signals-and-systems / [[LTI Systems]]

---
**Q.** What is a Bode plot?
?
A Bode plot is a graphical representation of the frequency response of a linear time-invariant system. It consists of two plots: one for the magnitude of the transfer function and another for the phase angle.

#signals-and-systems / [[Frequency Response]]

---
**Q.** What is a root locus?
?
A root locus plot is a graphical method for examining how the roots of a system change with variation of a certain system parameter, typically a gain in a feedback system.

#control-systems / [[Concept and Definition of Root Locus]]

**Q.** What is Gauss's law for electric fields?
?
Gauss's law for electric fields states that the net electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the total electric charge enclosed within that surface, divided by the permittivity of free space.

#electromagnetic-fields / [[Gauss's Law]]

---
**Q.** What is Ampere's circuital law?
?
Ampere's circuital law relates the integrated magnetic field around a closed loop to the electric current passing through the loop.

#electromagnetic-fields / [[Ampere's Law]]

---
**Q.** What is Faraday's law of induction?
?
Faraday's law of induction is a basic law of electromagnetism that predicts how a magnetic field will interact with an electric circuit to produce an electromotive force (EMF).

#electromagnetic-fields / [[Faraday's Law]]

**Q.** What is the difference between a synchronous motor and an induction motor?
?
A synchronous motor runs at a constant synchronous speed, regardless of the load, while the speed of an induction motor varies with the load. Synchronous motors require a separate DC excitation for the rotor, whereas induction motors do not.

#electrical-machines / [[Synchronous Machines]], [[Induction Motors]]

**Q.** What is the purpose of a circuit breaker?
?
A circuit breaker is an automatic electrical switch designed to protect an electrical circuit from damage caused by excess current from an overload or short circuit.

#power-system / [[Circuit Breakers]]

**Q.** What are symmetrical components?
?
Symmetrical components are a mathematical tool used to analyze unbalanced three-phase power systems. An unbalanced set of phasors is resolved into three balanced sets of phasors, known as the positive, negative, and zero sequence components.

#power-system / [[Symmetrical Components]]

---
**Q.** What is the purpose of a protective relay?
?
A protective relay is a device that detects a fault condition in an electrical circuit and initiates the operation of a circuit breaker to isolate the faulted part of the circuit.

#power-system / [[Protective Relays]]

---
**Q.** What is the difference between a star and a delta connection?
?
A star (or wye) connection is a three-phase connection where the ends of the three windings are connected to a common point. A delta connection is a three-phase connection where the three windings are connected in series to form a closed loop.

#electric-circuits / [[Three-Phase Circuits]]

---
**Q.** What is a semiconductor?
?
A semiconductor is a material that has an electrical conductivity value falling between that of a conductor, such as copper, and an insulator, such as glass.

#electric-circuits / [[Semiconductors]]

---
**Q.** What is doping in semiconductors?
?
Doping is the process of intentionally introducing impurities into an intrinsic semiconductor to change its electrical properties.

#electric-circuits / [[Semiconductors]]

---
**Q.** What are extrinsic and intrinsic semiconductors?
?
An intrinsic semiconductor is a pure semiconductor without any significant dopant species present. An extrinsic semiconductor is a semiconductor that has been doped with impurities to modify the number of free electrons and holes.

#electric-circuits / [[Semiconductors]]

---
**Q.** What is a p-n junction?
?
A p-n junction is a boundary or interface between two types of semiconductor materials, p-type and n-type, inside a single crystal of semiconductor. It is the basic building block of most semiconductor devices.

#electric-circuits / [[Semiconductors]]

---
**Q.** What is the skin effect?
?
The skin effect is the tendency of an alternating electric current to become distributed within a conductor such that the current density is largest near the surface of the conductor and decreases with greater depths in the conductor.

#electromagnetic-fields / [[Skin Effect]]

---
**Q.** What is the Corona effect in transmission lines?
?
The Corona effect is a phenomenon that occurs when the air surrounding a high-voltage transmission line conductor becomes ionized, leading to a violet glow, a hissing sound, and power loss.

#power-system / [[Transmission Lines]]

---
**Q.** What is Ferranti effect?
?
The Ferranti effect is the increase in voltage that occurs at the receiving end of a long transmission line, above the voltage at the sending end, when the line is lightly loaded or under no-load conditions.

#power-system / [[Transmission Lines]]

---
**Q.** What is the purpose of a damper winding in a synchronous machine?
?
Damper windings are used in synchronous machines to dampen oscillations and to provide starting torque in synchronous motors.

#electrical-machines / [[Synchronous Machines]]

---
**Q.** What are the different losses in a transformer?
?
The main losses in a transformer are core losses (hysteresis and eddy current losses) and copper losses (I²R losses) in the windings.

#electrical-machines / [[Transformers]]

---
**Q.** What is the voltage regulation of a transformer?
?
The voltage regulation of a transformer is the percentage change in its secondary terminal voltage from no-load to full-load at a given power factor.

#electrical-machines / [[Transformers]]

---
**Q.** What is the all-day efficiency of a transformer?
?
The all-day efficiency of a transformer is the ratio of the energy output in a 24-hour period to the energy input over the same period. It is particularly important for distribution transformers that are loaded intermittently.

#electrical-machines / [[Transformers]]

---
**Q.** What is a linear system?
?
A linear system is a system that possesses the property of superposition, meaning that the output of the system due to a sum of inputs is the sum of the outputs due to each input individually.

#signals-and-systems / [[LTI Systems]]

---
**Q.** What is a time-invariant system?
?
A time-invariant system is a system whose output response does not depend on when the input is applied. A shift in the input signal causes an identical shift in the output signal.

#signals-and-systems / [[LTI Systems]]

---
**Q.** What is the impulse response of a system?
?
The impulse response of a system is its output when the input is a unit impulse function. It completely characterizes a linear time-invariant (LTI) system.

#signals-and-systems / [[LTI Systems]]

---
**Q.** What is convolution?
?
Convolution is a mathematical operation on two functions that produces a third function expressing how the shape of one is modified by the other. In the context of LTI systems, the output is the convolution of the input with the system's impulse response.

#signals-and-systems / [[Continuous-Time Convolution Integral|Convolution]]

---
**Q.** What are poles and zeros of a transfer function?
?
Poles are the values of the complex variable 's' for which the transfer function goes to infinity, and zeros are the values of 's' for which the transfer function goes to zero. The locations of poles and zeros determine the stability and response of a system.

#control-systems / [[Poles and Zeros]]

**Q.** What is a state-space representation?
?
State-space representation is a mathematical model of a physical system as a set of input, output, and state variables related by first-order differential equations.

#control-systems / [[State-Space Representation of LTI Systems]]

**Q.** What is controllability and observability?
?
Controllability is the ability of an external input to move the internal state of a system from any initial state to any other final state in a finite time interval. Observability is the ability to determine the internal state of a system by observing its external outputs.
